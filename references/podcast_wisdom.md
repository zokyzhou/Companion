# Podcast wisdom — follow-up questions & methods (local, pick-up-and-use)

Curated, paraphrased methodology and reflective questions inspired by popular
mindfulness / purpose podcasts. Use these as **follow-up questions** and gentle
frames during a session — especially in *Explore why* and *Reframe / support*.

Rules:
- **Adapt, don't quote at them.** Weave a question in naturally, in your own
  warm words. Don't say "as Jay Shetty says…" unless it genuinely helps.
- **These are inspiration, not clinical advice.** The safety boundary in
  `safety.md` always wins.
- **One at a time.** Pick the single question that fits what they just said.
- This is the **offline** source. For something fresh/current, you may also do a
  live web search (see "Live search" at the bottom and in `SKILL.md`).

---

## Jay Shetty — *On Purpose* (purpose, identity, self-kindness)

Core methods:
- **You are not your thoughts.** Notice a thought without letting it define you;
  challenge the inner critic with actual evidence.
- **Reframe with intention** — treat the start of the day / this moment as the
  "operating system" that colors everything after.
- **Move through hard thoughts** with clarity and self-kindness, one at a time —
  not by forcing positivity.

Follow-up questions to borrow:
- "If nothing changed and you were living this same situation five years from
  now — how would that sit with you?"
- "Are you living your own life here, or performing a script someone else wrote
  for you?"
- "Whose voice is that inner critic, really? Is what it's saying actually true?"
- "What would the wisest, kindest version of you say about this?"
- "What's this feeling trying to protect or point you toward?"

## Mel Robbins — *The Mel Robbins Podcast* (control, action, expert tools)

Core methods:
- **The "Let Them" theory** — when you're spinning about what others do or how
  things "should" go, let them. Then **"Let Me"** — bring focus back to what
  *you* control: your response, your next step. Great for anxiety rooted in
  other people or uncertainty.
- **Name the alarm in the body** (from anxiety experts she hosts, e.g. Dr. Russ
  Kennedy) — locate the physical sensation of anxiety and name it, which loosens
  its grip.
- **Small, science-backed action** to shift state fast (e.g. a 5-4-3-2-1 count
  to interrupt a spiral and take one tiny step).

Follow-up questions to borrow:
- "What part of this is actually yours to control — and what could you let be?"
- "If you let them do their thing… what would *you* choose to do next?"
- "Where do you feel this in your body right now? Can we just name it together?"
- "What's one small, doable thing that would make the next hour 5% easier?"
- "Is this a problem to solve right now, or a feeling to sit with for a moment?"

---

## Matching guide (which follow-up when)

- Spinning about other people / "should" → **Let Them / Let Me**.
- Harsh inner critic / identity / stuck → **You are not your thoughts** +
  "whose voice is that?"
- Anxiety felt strongly in the body → **name the alarm in the body**.
- Feeling directionless / questioning the path → **On Purpose** life questions.
- Frozen / overwhelmed → **one small doable action**.
