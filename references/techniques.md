# Technique library (spoken, conversational)

Pick ONE technique that fits the moment. Always ask permission first, keep it
short, and do it *with* the user, pausing for their responses. These are
support tools drawn from mindfulness and CBT — not clinical treatment.

Match technique to state:
- Panicky / spiraling / dissociated → **Grounding** or **Breathing** first.
- Stuck in a harsh or catastrophic thought → **Reframing**.
- Unheard, needs to vent → **Reflective listening** (often enough on its own).
- Flat, low, depleted → **Gratitude / savoring** (gently, never toxic-positive).
- Can't name the feeling → **Naming / labeling**.

---

## 1. Naming / labeling ("name it to tame it")
Help them attach a precise word to the feeling. Offer a small menu.
> "If you had to name it — is it closer to anxious, sad, angry, or numb?"
Then reflect: "Okay — *disappointed*. That's a real one." Naming alone lowers
the charge. Optionally ask for intensity 0–10 to track over time.

## 2. Reflective listening (the default posture)
Mirror back what you heard, in your own words, before anything else.
> "So the deadline's looming and part of you is scared you'll let people down."
Then a single open door: "What else is under that?" Sometimes being truly heard
is the whole intervention — don't rush to fix.

## 3. 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (for anxiety / overwhelm / spiraling)
Bring them into the present through the senses. Go slowly, one at a time.
> "Let's ground for a sec. Look around — tell me five things you can see."
> (wait) "Nice. Now four things you can feel — your feet, the chair…"
> (wait) "Three things you can hear." → "Two you can smell." → "One you can
> taste, or one good thing about right now."
End: "Notice your feet on the floor. You're here, and you're safe right now."

## 4. Breathing together (box / slow exhale)
Regulate the nervous system with a longer exhale.
> "Let's take a few breaths together. Breathe in with me for four… hold two…
> and out, slow, for six." Do 3–4 rounds, counting with them. Longer out-breath
> than in-breath calms the body. Keep your voice slow and low.

## 5. Cognitive reframing (CBT-style, for harsh/distorted thoughts)
Gently loosen an all-or-nothing or catastrophic thought — as a question, never
a correction.
> "You said 'I always mess this up.' Can I gently poke at that? Is that always
> true — or is that the fear talking?"
Steps: (a) name the thought, (b) look for evidence for and against, (c) find a
kinder, truer version. "What might you say to a friend in this exact spot?"
Land on a reframe *they* generate, not one you impose.

## 6. Gratitude / savoring (for low, flat, depleted states)
Only after they feel heard — never to skip past pain.
> "When you're up for it — one small thing today that wasn't terrible? Even tiny
> counts." Let them find it. Then help them dwell on it for a beat.

## 7. Self-compassion break (for shame / self-criticism)
Three moves, spoken softly:
> "This is a moment of pain — that's hard, and it's human. Lots of people feel
> this. What would you say to someone you love who felt this way? Can you offer
> a bit of that to yourself right now?"

---

## Turn-taking reminders
- One technique per session unless they ask for more.
- Pause and wait after each prompt — let them actually do it.
- If a technique isn't landing, don't push. Drop it warmly and just listen.
- Always return to *them*: "How was that? Where are you now?"
